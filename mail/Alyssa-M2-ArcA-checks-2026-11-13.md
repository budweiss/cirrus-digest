Hi Alyssa,

Here are the checks for understanding for Module 2, Arc A (Lessons 1-7): four multiple-choice questions per lesson, drawn from each lesson's learning targets, vocabulary, and word study. Each question is tagged (target), (vocab), or (word study) so you can see what it checks. The answer key is at the end, so the question pages print clean. The Arc A lesson plans are in a separate email on your other thread, and Arcs B and C and the Module Finale will follow the same way.

----------------------------------------

LESSON 1 — Opening Bookend
Focus: activation: the Essential Question and the word endure, through students' own family and community stories

1. (vocab) In this module, a story that endures is a story that:
A. has no characters
B. is very short
C. continues to exist and be told for a long time
D. is told only once and then forgotten

2. (vocab) The word endure can also mean "to deal with something unpleasant for a long time." Which sentence uses endure that way?
A. That old song has endured for two hundred years.
B. The hikers had to endure three hours of cold rain.
C. Greek myths still endure today.
D. The story of our town has endured since it was founded.

3. (target) Our Essential Question is "What do people learn from myths and stories?" What is this question asking us to think about?
A. which story in the library is the longest
B. how to write a myth in one day
C. what lessons and knowledge stories pass on to the people who hear them
D. who drew the pictures in a book

4. (target) Why do families and communities pass stories down from one generation to the next?
A. Old stories are only for babies.
B. Stories are required by law.
C. Stories never teach anything.
D. Stories help people remember their history and share what matters to them.

----------------------------------------

LESSON 2 — Wonder
Focus: what makes a myth (Understanding Greek Myths) and noticing and wondering about Gifts from the Gods

1. (vocab) A myth is:
A. a news report about something that happened yesterday
B. a set of directions for a game
C. a list of facts about animals
D. a story told in an ancient culture to explain a practice, belief, or natural occurrence

2. (target) According to Understanding Greek Myths, which characters would you expect to find in a myth?
A. only scientists in a lab
B. only ordinary students at school
C. gods, goddesses, heroes, and monsters
D. only talking cars

3. (target) What are the two main types of Greek myths described in the excerpts?
A. short myths and long myths
B. funny myths and sad myths
C. creation myths and morality myths
D. true myths and false myths

4. (target) When a reader notices and wonders about a new book, the reader:
A. reads only the last page
B. looks at the illustrations and headings, finds unfamiliar words, and asks questions
C. skips the cover and the title
D. memorizes the page numbers

LESSON 3 — Organize
Focus: the elements of a myth (Nemesis), a written summary, and a first look at Winged Victory

1. (target) Who are the characters in the myth about Nemesis?
A. Nemesis, the goddess of justice; Croesus, a rich king; and Solon, a wise man
B. Achilles and his mother
C. Zeus, Hera, and Athena
D. Pandora and Epimetheus

2. (target) What does the myth about Nemesis explain?
A. People should always try to become rich.
B. The gods do not care what humans do.
C. People should not be too proud or brag about what they have.
D. Kings never make mistakes.

3. (target) Why is the myth about Nemesis a morality myth?
A. It takes place under the ocean.
B. It is very short.
C. It explains how the world was created.
D. It teaches people how to behave.

4. (word study) How is the word nemesis divided into syllables?
A. nem-es-is
B. nemes-is
C. ne-mesis
D. n-em-esis

----------------------------------------

LESSON 4 — Organize
Focus: summarizing a myth from the Jigsaw (oral summary) and describing Winged Victory

1. (target) A good summary of a myth includes:
A. every sentence from the myth
B. the characters, the setting, the main events, and what the myth explains
C. the reader's favorite part and nothing else
D. only the title

2. (target) In "Pandora's Box," what happens when Pandora opens the box?
A. She lets all the evil out into the world.
B. She finds a pile of gold.
C. She becomes a goddess.
D. Nothing happens.

3. (target) In "Achilles' Heel," why is Achilles' ankle the only part of him that can be hurt?
A. His mother dipped him in the River Styx, but his ankle stayed out of the water.
B. He broke his ankle when he was a baby.
C. He never wore shoes.
D. Zeus put a curse on his ankle.

4. (target) Which detail describes the sculpture Winged Victory?
A. The figure is a horse pulling a cart.
B. The figure is painted bright red.
C. The figure is sitting in a chair, holding a book.
D. The figure has large wings, leans forward, and wears a robe that looks like it is blowing in the wind.

LESSON 5 — Reveal
Focus: etymology (nemesis, fortune, victory) and the lines in Winged Victory

1. (vocab) Etymology is:
A. the study of insects
B. a kind of poem
C. a map of ancient Greece
D. the history of a word, or where a word came from

2. (vocab) The word fortune comes from Fortuna, the Roman goddess of luck. Which sentence uses fortune to mean "wealth; riches"?
A. We had bad fortune on our trip.
B. The inventor made a fortune selling her invention.
C. Fortune smiled on our team today.
D. It was good fortune that the rain stopped before the game.

3. (target) How does the myth of Victoria help you understand the word victory more deeply?
A. It shows that Victoria never watched any battles.
B. It shows that victory means losing.
C. It shows that victory is only about being the biggest.
D. It shows that winning meant having a just cause and fighting fairly, not only being strong.

4. (target) In Winged Victory, the curved lines in the robe help show:
A. that the figure is underwater
B. a sense of movement from the wind and the figure's body
C. that the sculpture is really a painting
D. that the figure is asleep

----------------------------------------

LESSON 6 — Reveal
Focus: the author's point and evidence in Gifts from the Gods, and how Winged Victory depicts victory

1. (vocab) The word illuminate contains the root lum, which means "light, glow." Which sentence uses illuminate to mean "to make something clear and easier to understand"?
A. Fireflies illuminate the yard at night.
B. The teacher's example helped illuminate the hard math problem.
C. The lamp will illuminate the dark room.
D. The candles illuminate the table.

2. (target) What point does the author make in Gifts from the Gods?
A. Myths can help people understand words and the world more deeply.
B. Only the gods can understand myths.
C. Words from Greek myths are no longer used today.
D. Myths are boring and should be forgotten.

3. (target) Which is one type of evidence the author uses to support that point?
A. math problems
B. recipes
C. weather reports
D. excerpts from modern books that use words from myths

4. (target) Which detail of Winged Victory best helps show the idea of victory?
A. The figure is hiding behind a rock.
B. The figure leans forward, ready for action, with large, powerful wings.
C. The figure is tiny and fragile.
D. The figure is lying down asleep.

----------------------------------------

LESSON 7 — Know
Focus: knowledge from Gifts from the Gods, "The Myth of Prometheus," and Winged Victory, stated with adjectives

1. (target) In "The Myth of Prometheus," what does Prometheus give to humans?
A. wings
B. fire
C. gold
D. a box

2. (target) How does Zeus punish Prometheus?
A. He sends him to live with the Muses.
B. He turns him into a tree.
C. He chains him to a cliff, where a vulture eats his liver every day.
D. He makes him the king of the gods.

3. (word study) Which word in this sentence is an adjective? "Prometheus was a clever Titan."
A. was
B. clever
C. Titan
D. Prometheus

4. (word study) Adjectives tell more about a noun by answering which questions?
A. how many?, what kind?, and which one?
B. where? and why?
C. how fast? and how loud?
D. who did it? and when?

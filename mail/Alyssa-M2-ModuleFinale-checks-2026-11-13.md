Here are the checks for understanding for the Module 2 Finale (Lessons 37-40): multiple-choice questions for each lesson, drawn from its learning targets, vocabulary, and word study, each tagged (target), (vocab), or (word study). The answer key is at the end, so the question pages print clean.

LESSON 37 — Module Finale, Know
Focus: what makes a story endure, the Socratic seminar, and finishing the End-of-Module Task draft

1. (vocab) When a story endures, it:
A. has no ending
B. is forgotten right away
C. is read only once
D. continues to exist and be shared for a long time

2. (target) Which is a reason a story might endure?
A. It teaches life lessons that still matter to people.
B. It was written very quickly.
C. It has no characters.
D. It is very hard to understand.

3. (target) Which is a reason The Invention of Hugo Cabret might be an enduring story?
A. It is only about trains.
B. It has no pictures.
C. It entertains readers and teaches that people can find their purpose.
D. It is the shortest book ever written.

4. (target) In a Socratic seminar, a good speaker:
A. builds on others' ideas and explains the evidence for their own
B. talks only to the teacher
C. changes the topic often
D. stays silent the whole time

----------------------------------------

LESSON 38 — Module Finale, Know
Focus: giving checklist-based feedback and recording a fluent reading of the End-of-Module Task

1. (target) Which is the most helpful feedback on a partner's narrative?
A. "I don't like it."
B. "Write more."
C. "I'd like to know what this character looks like."
D. "It's good."

2. (target) How does a checklist help when you give a partner feedback?
A. It shows which elements are strong and which are missing or need work.
B. It tells you how many words to write.
C. It picks the title for the story.
D. It shows who gets the highest score.

3. (word study) Reading with expression means:
A. reading in a whisper
B. skipping hard words
C. using your voice to show feeling
D. reading as fast as you can

4. (target) If you make a mistake while recording your story, a fluent reader:
A. pretends nothing happened and skips ahead
B. rereads the word or line to correct it
C. stops recording forever
D. starts reading a different story

----------------------------------------

LESSON 39 — Module Finale, Know
Focus: creating a drawing that enhances the End-of-Module Task narrative

1. (target) Which is one way an illustration can enhance a story?
A. It can remove the problem.
B. It can change the author's name.
C. It can make the story shorter.
D. It can show a character's feelings or actions.

2. (target) An illustration that creates suspense might show:
A. a page number
B. a list of the story's events
C. the title page
D. a character reaching for a door just as a shadow appears

3. (target) In The Invention of Hugo Cabret, the illustrations sometimes:
A. explain the rules of grammar
B. repeat the table of contents
C. add events that do not appear in the words
D. list the chapter titles

----------------------------------------

LESSON 40 — Closing Bookend
Focus: reflection on the module topic and the Essential Question

1. (target) What is our Essential Question for Module 2?
A. Who invented the movie camera?
B. What do people learn from myths and stories?
C. How do trains work?
D. Why do clocks need fixing?

2. (target) Which is one way people share stories that we studied in this module?
A. only phone calls
B. only text messages
C. only newspapers
D. films such as A Trip to the Moon

3. (target) What did we learn about why myths and stories endure?
A. They entertain, teach, and connect people across time and cultures.
B. They are only for adults.
C. They are always short.
D. They never change.

# Pennrose / Knight — 2026-27 Winter Outlook REFRESH (2026-07-27)

*Updates the 7/22 outlook with the latest ENSO data and reframes the season as a
probability distribution ("superforecasting" style) instead of a single range.
Basis: NOAA CPC ENSO advisory (Jul 2026) + the existing 50-yr PHL climatology
(`out/phl-climatology.md`) and driver analysis (`Snow-Drivers-Analysis.md`).*

## What changed since 7/22
- **The El Niño signal intensified.** On 7/22 we framed the winter as a "strong
  El Niño." As of NOAA's July advisory it's now tracking toward a **very strong
  ("super") event**: weekly Niño-3.4 already **+2.1 °C**, ONI (AMJ) **+0.98 °C**
  and rising, an **81% chance of a *very strong* El Niño in Oct-Dec** (would rank
  among the largest since 1950), and **97%+** odds it persists through the winter.
- **Direction of the takeaway is unchanged, but the variance is higher.** A
  bigger El Niño means more Pacific storm energy AND a stronger warm lean — which
  for Philadelphia's low elevation raises the rain/mix risk even as it raises the
  ceiling on any single coastal storm. In plain terms: **fewer but potentially
  bigger events, on a warmer base, with a wider range of outcomes.**

## Why we don't just raise the number
Our own 30-winter correlation work is the anchor here: **ENSO barely predicts our
snowfall (r ≈ +0.13).** What actually swings a Philly winter is **atmospheric
blocking** (−NAO / −AO): blocking winters average **~2× the snow** of non-blocking
ones (32.5″ vs 17.3″). Blocking is driven by the stratospheric polar vortex / SSW
timing, which has **low seasonal predictability** and is **unknowable in July.**
So a stronger El Niño widens the distribution — it doesn't move the center much.

## 2026-27 Philadelphia — probabilistic estimate
Anchored to the 50-yr record (mean **22.2″**, median **18″**, σ **16.8″**, ~5.4
events; last decade ~15″) and the super-El-Niño composite (avg ~16″ but the
**highest-variance, most boom-or-bust** category — 1982-83 ≈ 38″, 2015-16 ≈ 28″,
1997-98 < 1″; the record's biggest single storms all hit strong-El-Niño winters).

| Scenario | Seasonal total (PHL) | Rough odds | Archetype |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bust** (warm torch / storms go to rain, no blocking) | < 8″ | ~15-20% | 1997-98 |
| **Below-to-near normal** (central case) | 10-24″ | ~50% | typical recent winter |
| **Big season** (a blocking window lines up with a coastal storm) | 30-50″+ | ~20-25% | 2009-10 / 2015-16 / 1982-83 |

- **Central estimate:** below-to-near-normal, **~12-22″**, across **~3-5 snow
  events + ~2-4 ice/mix events** (the warm lean adds freezing-rain/mix days).
- **Shape of the risk:** right-skewed with a real floor. The *expected* total sits
  in the high-teens/low-20s, but the **median is lower (~16-18″)** because a few
  boom years pull the average up. Translation: **most likely a modest winter, with
  a meaningfully elevated tail risk of one blockbuster Nor'easter — and a real, if
  smaller, chance of a near-bust.**

## What it means for Bill (pricing + planning)
- **The per-event + seasonal-minimum structure matters more this year, not less.**
  A higher-variance season is exactly the case where flat seasonal pricing burns
  someone: you're exposed in a boom, they overpay in a bust. Per-event bills track
  reality; the seasonal minimum protects a quiet winter.
- **Plan crews/salt to the central case, stage for the tail.** Keep the 30-site
  planning baseline (~4 plow visits, ~4 sidewalk clears, ~5 salt apps → ~12.4 tons
  total). But a boom scenario could run **~1.5-2× that** on plow passes and salt, so
  it's worth pre-agreeing a **salt-surge / extra-push rate** now rather than mid-storm.
- **Rain-vs-snow line is the wildcard to watch.** In a warm super-El-Niño, borderline
  events may fall as mix/ice at Philly elevation — which shifts work from plowing
  toward **salting/ice management.** Worth making sure the ice/salt rate is dialed in.

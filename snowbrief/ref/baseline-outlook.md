## Why we still don't just raise the number
Our 30-winter correlation work remains the anchor: **ENSO barely predicts Philadelphia snowfall (r ≈ +0.13).** What swings a Philly winter is **blocking (-NAO/-AO)**: blocking winters average **~2× the snow** of non-blocking ones (32.5″ vs 17.3″). Blocking is driven by the stratospheric polar vortex / SSW timing — partly predictable only weeks out, and not reliably readable until the autumn precursors (October Siberian snow cover, Arctic sea ice, vortex formation) show up, with real confidence only December-February. A stronger El Niño widens the distribution and raises the single-storm ceiling; it doesn't move the center much. 1997-98 — a record El Niño that delivered 0.8″ — remains the standing warning.

Anchored to the 50-yr record (mean **22.2″**, median **18″**, σ **16.8″**, ~5.4 events; last decade ~15″) and the strong-El-Niño composite (avg ~16″, highest-variance, boom-or-bust: 1982-83 ≈ 38″, 2015-16 ≈ 28″, 1997-98 < 1″).

- **Central estimate:** below-to-near-normal, **~12-22″**, across **~3-5 snow events + ~2-4 ice/mix events**.
- **Shape of the risk:** right-skewed with a real floor — expected total in the high-teens/low-20s, median lower (~16-18″). Most likely a modest winter, with a meaningfully elevated tail risk of one blockbuster Nor'easter — and a real, if smaller, chance of a near-bust.
